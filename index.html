Notes on Statutory Architecture & Methodology

Primary Source: Suzanne Lazicki (Lucid Professional Writing) & DOS Visa Office

1. Statutory Set-Asides (RIA 2022)

Under the EB-5 Reform and Integrity Act of 2022, Public Law 117-103, 32% of the worldwide ~10,000 EB-5 visa quota is reserved: 20% for Rural areas, 10% for High Unemployment Areas (HUA), and 2% for Infrastructure projects. If reserved visas are unissued at fiscal year-end, they roll into the reserved pool for the following fiscal year before spilling into the unreserved category.

2. The 7.1% Per-Country Ceiling

INA § 202(a)(2) mandates that applicants from any single foreign state cannot exceed 7.1% of total family-sponsored and employment-based visas. In oversubscribed queues (such as India and China), this hard cap restricts annual reserved availability unless worldwide demand is completely cleared, precipitating retrogression in the monthly Department of State Visa Bulletin.

3. Concurrent Adjustment Mechanics

The RIA enacted INA § 245(m), allowing investors and qualifying dependents lawfully present in the United States under nonimmigrant status (e.g. F-1, H-1B, L-1, O-1) to file Form I-485 concurrently with Form I-526E. This provides immediate status continuity, independent employment authorization, and advance parole travel eligibility regardless of priority date backlogs.
